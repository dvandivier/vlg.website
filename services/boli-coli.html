For banks

Bank-owned life insurance (BOLI)

Permanent life insurance a bank buys on its officers or employees, with their consent. The bank pays the premium, owns the policy, and is the beneficiary. Banks often use it to help offset the rising cost of employee benefits.

For businesses

Company-owned life insurance (COLI)

The same idea for businesses that aren't banks, from manufacturers to medical practices. Companies use it for key-person protection, funding executive benefits, buy-sell agreements, and offsetting benefit costs.

Why older policies deserve a second look

These policies often sit quietly on the books for years without a real review.

Rates drift

What your policies earn can fall behind what newer products offer, and it rarely gets flagged on its own.

Designs age

Policies bought 10 or 20 years ago were built with the options of their time. The market has changed since.

Paperwork goes missing

Employee notice and consent forms on older policies can be hard to find. A review is a good time to confirm they're on file.

Common questions

What is BOLI?

Bank-owned life insurance is permanent life insurance a bank buys on a group of its officers or employees, with their written consent. The bank pays the premium, owns the policy, and is the beneficiary.

What is COLI, and how is it different?

Company-owned life insurance works the same way. The only difference is the owner: COLI is held by a business that isn't a bank.

Do we have to change agents to get a review?

No. A review does not require a change of agent of record. You see the results and decide what, if anything, to do next.

Is the cash value or death benefit taxable?

Cash value growth is generally tax-deferred, and the death benefit is generally income tax-free when the notice and consent rules are followed. Surrendering a policy can create taxable income. Confirm how the rules apply to you with your CPA or tax professional.
